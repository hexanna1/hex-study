Dark Hex

Repository with strategies, proofs, and code

Standard Dark Hex is solved on 3×4 and 4×4 boards. With optimal play, Red wins about 21.16% and 79.34% of the time, respectively.

The exact probabilities are 14279 / 67484 for 3×4 and 5345119702052171 / 6736824633547900 for 4×4. The result comes with optimal randomized strategies for both players. Exact best-response checks against those strategies establish matching lower and upper bounds. For 3×4, the previous published bound was 1/7 to 1/4.

The game

Board sizes are given as columns × rows. Red connects top to bottom; Blue connects left to right. Players see their own stones and remember their own probes, but cannot see the opponent's stones. If a probe hits an occupied cell, it fails privately and the player tries again. A successful placement ends the turn. Red's first move is hidden from Blue.

The strategies

The published strategies give probabilities for the next probe at each private history. Explore either player's 4×4 strategy from the GitHub certificates. An optimal strategy guarantees the game's value, but may make weaker moves in histories that do not affect that guarantee.

Public openings

In this variant, Red's first stone is announced before Blue moves. Every opening is solved. The boards show Red's win probability (%) for each announced opening, assuming optimal play afterward.

On 3×4, b2 and b3 are the best announced openings. Red's win probability falls from 21.16% to 14.29%, a drop of 6.87 percentage points.

On 4×4, d1 and a4 are best. Red's win probability falls from 79.34% to 74.02%, a drop of 5.32 percentage points.
